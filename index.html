05 / The analysis protocol

Reverse-engineer the scene

Pick one important scene or decision and treat it like a case study. Separate what the character actually knew from what the story lets the audience know, then test whether the reasoning survives in real life.

Fiction gives characters perfect timing, hidden information, or plot-level foresight. Your goal is to extract realistic, ethical skills — not imitate impossible feats. Use the six steps as a repeatable analysis loop.

After each pass, ask: what would I need to know, in real life, to reach the same conclusion? Then write the rule you can actually apply.

01Observe. What facts, signals, and constraints did the character actually notice?
02Reconstruct. What assumptions and hypotheses connected the evidence to the conclusion?
03Compare. What alternative explanations or actions were available?
04Test. Try the same reasoning on a real problem or practice exercise.
05Evaluate. What was correct, uncertain, biased, or simply lucky?
06Transfer. Write one rule you can use in a different subject or situation.
06 / The doctrine

How to use this curriculum properly

1. One main lesson at a time

Start with the first selected lesson in a topic. Do not collect extra courses before finishing the core material.

2. Produce an output for every topic

Create an argument evaluation, logic exercise, hypothesis, system map, decision comparison, observation log, or reflection.

3. Practise retrieval

Close the lesson and explain the main ideas from memory. Revisit them after a delay.

4. Keep a mistake journal

Record what you got wrong, why it happened, and what you will try differently next time.

5. Apply the skill to everyday life

Use these skills to understand information, solve ordinary problems, make decisions, learn, and communicate.

6. Don't imitate fictional flaws

Keep useful habits. Reject cruelty, manipulation, superiority, emotional suppression, and impossible standards. Characters are inspiration — not scientific authorities.

Curriculum boundary: this is a general intellectual-development curriculum covering reasoning, strategy, psychology, learning, decisions, and personal planning. The anime characters are illustrative lenses; actual methods are taught through the linked educational sources.
07 / The workbook

Templates — fill these in, don't just read them

Lessons give you input. These templates are the accountability loop. Copy one per session.

Mistake journal

After every practical output. Be specific — what exactly was off, and what will you try next time?

Weekly review

Every 7 days. Look for patterns across your mistake-journal entries.

Spaced re-check schedule

For each completed module, re-test yourself from memory — no notes — at roughly these intervals:

Day 1 → Day 3 → Day 7 → Day 21

If you can't reconstruct the core idea + your practical output at a checkpoint, that module isn't done — redo the output, not the reading.

Rubric: is this module actually done?

Score 1 (no) to 3 (yes) on each:

(1) I produced the practical output without copying the lesson's example.

(2) I passed the module's mastery-test question from memory.

(3) I can give one real-life example from this week where I used the skill.

7–9 = done. Below 7 = redo the output, not the reading.

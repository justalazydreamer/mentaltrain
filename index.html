02 / THE TRAINING SYSTEM

16 core skills. One clear learning path.

These are the exact 16 general intellectual skills from your list. Study them in order, use the selected free lessons, and complete the practical output before moving on.

SCOPE: general thinking, psychology, learning, decision-making, and personal planning.
01

Critical Thinking

SKILL 01LEvaluate claims before accepting them

Train: Evaluate claims by checking the reasons, evidence, assumptions, source quality, and strongest counterargument. Critical thinking is the broad habit of judging how well an idea is supported.

80/20 concepts

Claims vs evidencePremises & conclusionsHidden assumptionsFallacies & biasSource evaluationCounterarguments
Practical output: Choose one claim from a news story, video, or conversation. Write the claim, its evidence, its hidden assumptions, and the strongest fair objection. Decide how confident you should be.

Start here — selected lessons

Start with the introductory critical-thinking and argument-analysis sections.↗UNSW — Critical ThinkingA practical university guide to analysis, questioning assumptions, evaluating evidence, and considering alternative explanations.↗
Mastery test: Can you explain why a claim is strong or weak using evidence and reasoning instead of attacking the person who made it?

Formal Logic

SKILL 02LCheck whether conclusions really follow

03

08

Direct observationInterpretation vs factContext & prior expectationsInference strengthAlternative explanationsVerification
Practical output: Observe a scene, event, or short interaction. Make two columns: “What I directly observed” and “What I inferred.” Mark which inferences are uncertain and what additional evidence could check them.

05 / The doctrine

How to use this curriculum properly

1. One main lesson at a time

Start with the first selected lesson in a topic. Do not collect extra courses before finishing the core material.

2. Produce an output for every topic

Create an argument evaluation, logic exercise, hypothesis, system map, decision comparison, observation log, or reflection.

3. Practise retrieval

Close the lesson and explain the main ideas from memory. Revisit them after a delay.

4. Keep a mistake journal

Record what you got wrong, why it happened, and what you will try differently next time.

5. Apply the skill to everyday life

Use these skills to understand information, solve ordinary problems, make decisions, learn, and communicate.

6. Don't imitate fictional flaws

Keep useful habits. Reject cruelty, manipulation, superiority, emotional suppression, and impossible standards. Fictional characters are inspiration—not scientific authorities.

Curriculum boundary: This is a general intellectual-development curriculum. It covers reasoning, strategy, psychology, learning, decisions, and personal planning. The anime characters are illustrative lenses; actual methods are taught through the linked educational sources.
